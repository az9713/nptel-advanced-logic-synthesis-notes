# 6. Design environment, constraints, compile strategy, and reports

A mapped execution slice meets a cycle constraint only in a defined environment. The report must identify the operating point, scenario, external circuit assumptions, and paths analyzed. Tightening a valid constraint can change the circuit. Tightening a constraint whose targets are empty only changes the script.

## Model the surroundings and the hierarchy [L16 12:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7yRJREMrl3s&t=720s) · [L16 41:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7yRJREMrl3s&t=2467s)

A design definition can be instantiated multiple times. Optimizing one instance's context can require distinct definitions when another instance sees different loads or timing budgets. **Uniquification** creates those distinct definitions. Removing hierarchy exposes logic on both sides of a boundary and may reduce duplication or depth. Retaining hierarchy helps reuse, path identification, and block ownership. When a block is synthesized independently, exchange the pin transition, load, arrival, and required values consistent with its top-level environment.

Electrical assumptions include input driving characteristics and output capacitance. Before placement, a legacy **wire-load model** estimates net resistance and capacitance from quantities such as fanout and selected hierarchy area. The model cannot know where this decoder's consumers will land. For example, if synthesis assumes $8$ fF wire load and placement creates $18$ fF, that extra $10$ fF at $2$ kΩ effective drive adds about $14$ ps in a single-pole 50% model. Physical estimation improves the input to optimization, and extracted parasitics later describe a specific routed implementation.

The lecture's legacy **top** mode uses the top design's model for all nets. **Enclosed** mode uses the model of the smallest hierarchy that encloses the entire net. **Segmented** mode applies each hierarchy's model to the corresponding part of a crossing net. Thus a net entirely inside the arithmetic block may be estimated from that block in enclosed mode, while a net spanning that block and its sibling is estimated at their enclosing parent. Check the reported model and use early physical data when the flow has it. [L16 41:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7yRJREMrl3s&t=2467s)

## Separate electrical rules from scheduling budgets [L17 06:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_ewT68b8gLk&t=363s) · [L17 36:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_ewT68b8gLk&t=2195s)

Timing constraints describe when data is launched and required, while electrical limits bound quantities such as load and transition. A clock period defines repeated edge relationships. Input and output delays reserve time for neighboring blocks and board-level behavior. A virtual clock is a reference waveform with no physical source object in the block, useful for interfaces timed relative to an external domain. Define minimum and maximum input/output delays when both setup and hold must be checked. Sign conventions, especially at output for the downstream hold requirement, must follow the tool's definition.

Consider a single-clock slice with period $T=1.0$ ns. An input has latest arrival $0.18$ ns after the reference edge. A downstream interface reserves $0.22$ ns before the next edge and uncertainty reserves $0.05$ ns, so the internal maximum delay budget is $1.0-0.18-0.22-0.05=0.55$ ns under this simplified contract. If the preceding block becomes $0.07$ ns later, the internal budget drops to $0.48$ ns. A driver model change may also increase slew and alter actual delay, independently of that schedule change. Check expected ports and clocks by querying the resolved collections. Review timing coverage after changes, including cases where the intended clock is absent. [OpenSTA command examples](https://github.com/The-OpenROAD-Project/OpenSTA/blob/master/doc/Examples.md)

Derive the minimum output constraint from an external hold check. With a shared reference edge and zero external wire delay, suppose the downstream register requires its input to stay stable for 40 ps after capture. The block output must then hold its old value through +40 ps. In SDC's external-delay convention this is an output `-min` of −40 ps because the required minimum arrival is the reference time minus the specified output delay. If the external path contributes 10 ps of minimum delay, the block only has to hold through +30 ps. Confirm the signs against the downstream timing report. Chapter 9 works through the corresponding internal hold equation. ## Choose a compile strategy and preserve comparison baselines [L18 06:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PA5KzTcLIhQ&t=404s) · [L18 40:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PA5KzTcLIhQ&t=2430s)

Top-down synthesis exposes cross-module logic to one optimizer and propagates top-level constraints. Bottom-up synthesis allows smaller blocks to compile independently. It depends on the accuracy of each boundary budget. A mixed flow can characterize blocks under the parent context, compile them separately, reassemble, and perform incremental work where timing still fails. For two serial blocks with $0.55$ ns total budget, a $0.20/0.35$ ns split is feasible when their implementations take $0.18/0.32$ ns. A $0.35/0.20$ split flags the second block even though their combined delay is $0.50$ ns. Use the parent paths and boundary slew/load to decide whether to change the block or its allocation.

A tighter effort level, factoring, flattening, or arithmetic restructuring can change mapped design size and path shape. Compare with an explicit baseline and retain the exact inputs, constraints, libraries, tool version, and reports. Incremental optimization starts from a mapped network and concentrates on its remaining violations. If the architecture or constraints change substantially, a clean compile offers a meaningful comparison. Rebinding or renaming objects can invalidate saved constraints, so reapply and check collections before reading a revised result.

The lecture also treats ideal networks. Use an ideal clock or similar explicitly modeled network only while its distribution delay and electrical behavior are supplied by the abstraction. It can suppress ordinary timing and design-rule optimization of the network. Switching to propagated clocks after clock-tree synthesis changes which elements contribute clock delay. Review source latency, propagated network latency, and uncertainty together to avoid double counting. [L18 25:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PA5KzTcLIhQ&t=1540s)

## Reconcile reports with the design contract [L19 16:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sWmMCh-bRFo&t=1000s) · [L19 33:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sWmMCh-bRFo&t=2023s)

For endpoint slacks $-80,-25,+12$ ps, the minimum slack is $-80$ ps. Total negative slack (TNS) for this one-value-per-endpoint example is $-105$ ps, and two endpoints violate. If a change yields $-60,-10,-10$ ps, the worst deficit shrinks, TNS improves to $-80$ ps, and the violating-endpoint count grows to three. Compare the same check type, endpoint population, and scenario. A tool's worst-negative-slack metric may report zero when all paths pass, while a worst-slack report can show the positive minimum. Follow the worst endpoint through launch clock, cell delay, net delay, slew, capture clock, and check value. For power and area inspect which hierarchy and constraints produced the totals. [L19 23:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sWmMCh-bRFo&t=1401s)

Electrical limits can be violated on paths that have positive timing slack. A poor transition can propagate inaccurate estimates or fail the characterized envelope. Check transition, capacitance, fanout, missing clock, missing I/O delay, and unconstrained endpoint reports, then compare the timing summary. A signoff report and a synthesis estimate can legitimately disagree when parasitics, clocks, scenarios, or characterized libraries differ.

## Laboratory and solved problems

`python code/lab.py constraints` checks the boundary budget and endpoint aggregation. The data are fabricated for controlled comparison. Use the same accounting on the course's reports before any attempt to reproduce its tool results.

1. **Which compile is preferable given only the two slack lists?** The second reduces worst and total deficit but spreads violations to an additional endpoint. If every endpoint must meet setup, both fail. Compare area and hold under the same scenarios and determine whether the new endpoint is easy to repair before selecting a candidate.
2. **A bottom-up block has 100 ps spare at one output while the parent still fails through that pin. How is that possible?** The block may have been compiled with a required time 150 ps looser than the parent can offer. The block's 100 ps local spare then leaves a 50 ps parent deficit, before other effects. Compare timing at the same reference edge and reconcile observed load and slew.
